# 21st-Century Student Skills Framework v1.0

A lightweight framework for evaluating student readiness across cross-cutting competencies that apply in any discipline. Use it alongside, not instead of, subject-specific rubrics.

## Competencies

- **Critical Thinking** — analyzing problems, evaluating evidence, and making reasoned judgments.
- **Collaboration** — working effectively with others, including across disciplines, cultures, and remote or asynchronous settings.
- **Communication** — expressing ideas clearly in writing, speaking, and visual forms; listening and adapting to audience.
- **Creativity** — generating, combining, and refining original ideas, approaches, or artifacts.
- **Digital Literacy** — effective and responsible use of digital tools, data, and information environments.
- **Self-Direction** — identifying goals, planning work, managing time, and learning independently.
- **Domain Depth** — demonstrated command of the content, methods, and professional standards of the student's chosen field.

## Proficiency scale

Use these level names verbatim. Every framework competency receives exactly one level per student.

- **Emerging** — beginning to demonstrate the skill, usually with scaffolding or in guided contexts.
- **Developing** — applies the skill in familiar situations, with occasional guidance.
- **Proficient** — consistently applies the skill independently in routine contexts of study or work.
- **Advanced** — applies the skill reliably in novel or complex contexts, including teaching or leading others.
- **Not Demonstrated** — no evidence of this skill appears in the student's profile.

## Evidence guidance

When assigning a level, cite specific items from the student's profile (skills, certifications, awards, projects, experience, education).

- **Critical Thinking** — look for projects involving analysis, research, or evaluation; coursework in logic, research methods, or advanced seminars; essay or research awards.
- **Collaboration** — team projects or competitions; club or student-government leadership; peer-tutoring, teaching assistantships, or mentoring roles; ensemble work in arts.
- **Communication** — presentations, publications, writing awards, public-speaking roles, multilingual skills with context, student-journalism work.
- **Creativity** — original projects, artistic portfolios, patents, inventions, unusual cross-disciplinary combinations, hackathon wins.
- **Digital Literacy** — programming or data skills, productivity or design tools, certifications in digital platforms, projects using digital research or production methods.
- **Self-Direction** — self-taught skills, independent study, side projects unrelated to coursework, long-term personal commitments, self-designed majors.
- **Domain Depth** — advanced coursework, field-specific certifications, capstone or thesis research, internships or research assistantships in the field, subject-specific awards.

## Rating guidance

- Weight direct evidence (something the student *did*) more heavily than mentioned-skills lists.
- A single high-quality project can justify **Proficient** in one competency but cannot by itself justify **Advanced**.
- **Advanced** typically requires repeated or scaled evidence: multiple projects, leadership role, teaching others, or recognition from external judges.
- Reserve **Not Demonstrated** for competencies with zero relevant evidence; do not use it as a polite "not strong."
