## Common outcomes — §6.1 Common to all programs (a–e)

These 5 outcomes apply to every BSCS / BSIS / BSIT graduate and reflect the general-education and values attributes expected by CHED. Each is mapped (in §6.1 itself) to the sub-discipline-specific codes in parentheses.

- **common-po-1** *(§6.1a · maps to CS10 / IS10 / IT13)* — Articulate and discuss the latest developments in the specific field of practice (Philippine Qualifications Framework level 6 descriptor).
- **common-po-2** *(§6.1b · maps to CS08 / IS08 / IT10)* — Effectively communicate orally and in writing using both English and Filipino.
- **common-po-3** *(§6.1c · maps to CS07 / IS07 / IT08)* — Work effectively and independently in multi-disciplinary and multi-cultural teams (PQF level 6 descriptor).
- **common-po-4** *(§6.1d · maps to CS09 / IS09 / IT12)* — Act in recognition of professional, social, and ethical responsibility.
- **common-po-5** *(§6.1e · no sub-discipline mapping)* — Preserve and promote "Filipino historical and cultural heritage" (based on RA 7722).

## BS Computer Science — §6.3A (CS01–CS10)

10 outcomes covering computing theory, mathematics, and software design, grouped by graduate-attribute area.

- **bscs-po-1** *(CS01 · Knowledge for Solving Computing Problems)* — Apply knowledge of computing fundamentals, knowledge of a computing specialization, and mathematics, science, and domain knowledge appropriate for the computing specialization to the abstraction and conceptualization of computing models from defined problems and requirements.
- **bscs-po-2** *(CS02 · Problem Analysis)* — Identify, analyze, formulate, research literature, and solve complex computing problems and requirements reaching substantiated conclusions using fundamental principles of mathematics, computing sciences, and relevant domain disciplines.
- **bscs-po-3** *(CS03 · Design/Development of Solutions)* — Apply mathematical foundations, algorithmic principles, and computer-science theory in the modeling and design of computer-based systems in a way that demonstrates comprehension of the tradeoffs involved in design choices.
- **bscs-po-4** *(CS04 · Design/Development of Solutions)* — Knowledge and understanding of information-security issues in relation to the design, development, and use of information systems.
- **bscs-po-5** *(CS05 · Design/Development of Solutions)* — Design and evaluate solutions for complex computing problems, and design and evaluate systems, components, or processes that meet specified needs with appropriate consideration for public health and safety, cultural, societal, and environmental considerations.
- **bscs-po-6** *(CS06 · Modern Tool Usage)* — Create, select, adapt, and apply appropriate techniques, resources, and modern computing tools to complex computing activities, with an understanding of the limitations, to accomplish a common goal.
- **bscs-po-7** *(CS07 · Individual and Team Work)* — Work in diverse teams and multidisciplinary settings.
- **bscs-po-8** *(CS08 · Communication)* — Communicate effectively with the computing community and with society at large about complex computing activities by being able to comprehend and write effective reports, design documentation, make effective presentations, and give and understand clear instructions.
- **bscs-po-9** *(CS09 · Computing Professionalism and Ethics)* — Recognize the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues involved in the utilization of computer technology and be guided by the adoption of appropriate professional, ethical, and legal practices.
- **bscs-po-10** *(CS10 · Life-Long Learning)* — Engage in planning self-learning for continual development as a computing professional.

## BS Information Systems — §6.3B (IS01–IS10)

10 outcomes. BSIS is **not** an actively supported program in this analysis pipeline (FEU Tech's computing offerings are BSCS / BSIT / BSCpE), so these clauses exist for completeness and cross-walks. Wording below is OCR-derived where extracted clean; the rest follows the widely-circulated CHED-PSG template.

- **bsis-po-1** *(IS01)* — Apply knowledge of business processes, computing, mathematics, and social sciences appropriate to information systems.
- **bsis-po-2** *(IS02)* — Analyze a problem, identify and define the computing requirements with respect to organizational factors appropriate to its solution, and plan strategies for their solution.
- **bsis-po-3** *(IS03)* — Evaluate information systems in terms of general quality attributes and possible tradeoffs presented within the given requirement.
- **bsis-po-4** *(IS04)* — Design, implement, and evaluate information systems, processes, components, or programs, and source cost-benefit-efficient solutions.
- **bsis-po-5** *(IS05)* — Use knowledge and understanding of enterprises in modelling and design of information systems.
- **bsis-po-6** *(IS06 · Modern Tool Usage)* — Deploy and use effectively the skills, tools, and techniques necessary for information-systems practice.
- **bsis-po-7** *(IS07 · Individual and Team Work)* — Function effectively on teams (recognizing the different roles within a team and different ways of organizing teams) to accomplish a common goal.
- **bsis-po-8** *(IS08 · Communication)* — Communicate effectively with a range of audiences. Communication skills include technical writing, presentation and negotiation, and numeracy.
- **bsis-po-9** *(IS09 · Professionalism and Ethics)* — Recognize the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues involved in the exploitation of computer technology and be guided by the adoption of appropriate professional, ethical, and legal practices both in the local and global community.
- **bsis-po-10** *(IS10 · Life-Long Learning)* — Recognize the need for, and engage in, independent and life-long learning, planning self-learning and improving performance as the foundation for on-going professional development.

## BS Information Technology — §6.3C (IT01–IT13)

13 outcomes.

- **bsit-po-1** *(IT01)* — Apply knowledge of computing, science, and mathematics appropriate to the discipline.
- **bsit-po-2** *(IT02)* — Understand best practices and standards and their applications.
- **bsit-po-3** *(IT03)* — Analyze complex problems, and identify and define the computing requirements appropriate to their solution.
- **bsit-po-4** *(IT04)* — Identify and analyze user needs and take them into account in the selection, creation, evaluation, and administration of computer-based systems.
- **bsit-po-5** *(IT05)* — Design, implement, and evaluate computer-based systems, processes, components, or programs to meet desired needs and requirements under various constraints.
- **bsit-po-6** *(IT06)* — Integrate IT-based solutions into the user environment effectively.
- **bsit-po-7** *(IT07)* — Apply knowledge through the use of current techniques, skills, tools, and practices necessary for the IT profession.
- **bsit-po-8** *(IT08)* — Function effectively as a member or leader of a development team recognizing the different roles within a team to accomplish a common goal.
- **bsit-po-9** *(IT09)* — Assist in the creation of an effective IT project plan.
- **bsit-po-10** *(IT10)* — Communicate effectively with the computing community and with society at large about complex computing activities through logical writing, presentations, and clear instructions.
- **bsit-po-11** *(IT11)* — Analyze the local and global impact of computing information technology on individuals, organizations, and society.
- **bsit-po-12** *(IT12)* — Understand professional, ethical, legal, security, and social issues and responsibilities in the utilization of information technology.
- **bsit-po-13** *(IT13)* — Recognize the need for and engage in planning self-learning and improving performance as a foundation for continuing professional development.

## How to cite

Use the format `[ched-25:<clause-id>]`, e.g. `[ched-25:bscs-po-3]` or equivalently `[ched-25:cs03]`. Cite the *specific* program outcome the student's evidence supports — do not cite multiple clauses when one suffices.

When a profile's `program` is `BSCS`, prefer `bscs-po-*` (or `cs*`) clauses. When `BSIT`, prefer `bsit-po-*` (or `it*`). `common-po-*` clauses apply to every program.
